# Francis of Assisi: Without Gloss
## WisdomForge Booklet — Ages 15–18

---

## About This Book

You already know a Francis. He is usually wrong.

The birdbath Francis is a Layer-4 folk saint. The “instrument of your peace” Francis is a twentieth-century poem wearing a brown habit. The dropout Francis who “just loved Jesus and hated the Church” collides with a deacon who would adore the Eucharist in every church he could crawl into. The ecological Francis is more defensible — the Canticle is his — but only if you keep the Most High in the first line.

This booklet argues. It uses Francis’s own corpus (Armstrong and Brady, *Francis and Clare: The Complete Works*) as a veto over later lives. It uses Thomas of Celano (1228) as early, official, and already selective. It uses Augustine Thompson (2012) as a check on both. It will not resolve the stigmata in a lab. It will not pretend Damietta was an interfaith summit. It will not pretend poverty was a lifestyle brand.

Six sittings: leper and the social dead; Lady Poverty and coin; Rule, gloss, and institution; creation as vestige not idol; Damietta’s “or”; La Verna, Sister Death, and the long civil war over his will.

If you want a mascot, stop here. If you want a problem that still cuts, read on.

## Chapter 1: The Social Dead and the First Theology

You are trained to look for a conversion scene with weather: light, voice, collapse. Francis denies you that as the beginning. In the *Testament*, the first theology is a body he could not stand.

> “The Lord gave me, Brother Francis, thus to begin doing penance in this way: for when I was in sin, it seemed too bitter a thing to see lepers. And the Lord Himself led me among them and I showed mercy to them. And when I departed from them, what had seemed bitter to me was changed into sweetness of soul and body. And afterwards I delayed a little and left the world.”

Four movements: bitterness, being led, mercy, sweetness. Then delay. Then leaving. The sequence wrecks two modern sermons. One sermon says insight precedes action. Francis acts, then the palate changes. The other sermon says conversion is instantaneous. He *delays*. The seed is dated; the plant is not a firework.

Assisi’s leprosaria sat outside walls because medieval Christianity had fused illness with a purity code. Luke Demaitre’s medical history and the civil/canon practices of “social death” (property, marriage, church access) mean Francis is not performing a random kindness. He is crossing a legal and liturgical line. Touch is policy.

Kenneth Baxter Wolf asked whether the leper is a stage for a merchant’s son’s drama. Take the objection at full strength. Hagiography loves the kiss on the road; Francis does not narrate the kiss. He narrates mercy and a sensory inversion. That is less cinematic and harder to dismiss as cosplay. Still, a last will that makes a leper the origin of a world-famous order *does* use that body forever. Ethical reading holds both: the encounter is real solidarity *and* it becomes capital in a saint’s archive.

Collestrada (1202) and the Perugia prison are not the origin he chooses. They are conditions. Communal Italy taught him that walls change owners (imperial sack, 1174; commune revolt, 1198; papal orbit). A year with someone else’s keys can empty knighthood of glamour without teaching you whom to touch. The leper teaches whom to touch.

Celano will later give you San Damiano’s speaking crucifix as a set piece. Francis’s Testament does not. For this chapter, let the silence govern. The historical Francis, if we privilege Layer 1, locates God first in the person the town had declared a curse.

Hagiography is not one book. It is five jobs stacked on one man (Harry 7.2). Layer 1 is Francis’s own corpus: Canticle, Testament, Rules, letters, prayers — not a memoir, but a veto. Layer 2 is Thomas of Celano’s *Vita Prima* (1228–29), commissioned after canonization, already selecting for a saint, then *Vita Secunda* (1247) to correct and thicken. Layer 3 is Bonaventure’s *Legenda Maior* (1263): a Minister General’s unification, a coherent *alter Christus*, and the order to sideline messier earlier lives. Layer 4 is the *Fioretti* (late 14th century): wolf, birds, crib as folk devotion, more than a hundred years out. Layer 5 is Thompson and the *Early Documents* project, which treat the layers as evidence rather than as a single fresco. The closer you stand to Layer 1, the more anxious, sacramental, and un-cute the man. The farther you stand, the more the garden statue wins. Conversion is the test case. Celano needs a speaking crucifix and a kiss on the road. Francis, dying, names a leper and a delay. A reconstruction that lets Layer 2 overrule Layer 1 is not “richer.” It is a different beginning.

Scholarly debate: is conversion here “ethical” (Wolf, Thompson’s caution) or “mystical” (the later lives’ kiss and sweetness as miracle)? The *Testament*’s language is mystical *because* it is ethical: sweetness of *soul and body*. Dualism loses. So does a purely sociological reduction that cannot hear “the Lord led me.” The task is not to pick a campus. It is to refuse a Francis who never needed leading and a Francis who never needed a leper. Conservative readers (inside the order) hold that early narrative cores are sound even when miracles are later paint. Critical readers (Thompson) strip hagiographic jobs to recover a reformer. Hermeneutic readers (*Early Documents*) refuse the strip: perception is part of what Francis historically became. You need all three methods in the room. None of them gets to invent a saying.

The *Testament*’s theology of initiative is repetitive on purpose. “The Lord gave me” structures conversion, churches, brothers, Gospel form, priests, peace greeting. Matura reads this as vocation received, not self-authored. A high-school culture of personal branding will hear that as passivity. Francis’s life is not passive. He walks, kisses (if Celano is right), strips, sails, hides wounds. Received is not the opposite of strenuous. It is the opposite of sovereign. Keep that distinction when you write the Research Prompt: the speaking crucifix is Celano’s job; the leper is Francis’s.

Compare Paul on the Damascus road (Acts 9) only to see the difference. Paul gets a name-changing voice and blindness as sign. Francis gets a smell he used to flee and a sweetness he cannot argue himself into. If you prefer Paul’s weather, notice what you are avoiding: the poor body as the site of revelation.

Assisi’s *minores* had already tasted political victory in 1198. Francis’s refusal is therefore not the peasant’s necessity. It is the winner’s son walking away from the win. That is why Pietro cannot decode it as ordinary charity. Charity, in a merchant house, is display that leaves the shop standing. Francis unmakes the shop. Wolf’s suspicion (spiritual capital) and Francis’s verbs (led, showed mercy) have to share the page or you will get a tract. The Research Prompt stays on this chapter because the order of beginnings is the whole method of the booklet. Do not move it to La Verna. The speaking crucifix is the temptation of a better story. The leper is the document. Privilege Layer 1 or admit you are writing a different beginning. That is a historical method, not a piety. Keep the Research Prompt on chapter 1. Testament versus Celano is the method. Keep it. Do not relocate it. Layer 1 vetoes the set piece. The leper remains first.

### Big Idea

Francis’s theology starts in proximity to the socially dead. Divine initiative (“the Lord gave/led”) does not cancel embodiment; it runs through it. If your Francis begins with birds, you have skipped his first sentence.

### Practice

1. Two-column transcript: left, *Testament* leper paragraph copied by hand; right, a popular children’s version. Mark every addition. Write 200 words on what the additions are for.
2. Map “outside the walls” in your own institution (school, church, job). Name the bell — the signal that says “approach carefully.” One page, no hero story about yourself.
3. Read one chapter of Thompson on the conversion years. List three places he distrusts Celano. Do not pretend you now “know the real Francis.”
4. For one week, notice when you help from a safe height (money, post, pity) versus when you actually come close. Private log. No social media.

### Research Prompt

Compare Francis’s *Testament* (Armstrong/Brady) with Celano, *Vita Prima* 12–14 (San Damiano voice) and 17–25 (leper). Argue, in 750–1000 words, why a historical reconstruction should or should not let the speaking crucifix overrule the Testament’s order of beginnings. Use Thompson as a third voice. Cite pages. This is the one outside-reading task in the booklet.

## Chapter 2: Coin, Lady, and the Merchant’s Nerve

Lady Poverty is not a metaphor you can retire after the poetry unit. Francis hijacks *fin’amor* — total service to an unattainable lady — and aims it at the condition his class existed to escape. The scandal is class-specific. Pietro’s wealth is not feudal land. It is cloth, credit, routes, coin. Francis does not flee “the world” in general. He flees the new world that made his house.

The Later Rule, chapter 6, is the legal face of the romance:

> “And let the brothers not appropriate anything to themselves, neither house nor place nor anything. And as pilgrims and strangers in this world, serving the Lord in poverty and humility, let them go with confidence to seek alms.”

Pilgrim, stranger, confidence. Those nouns assume a God who still keeps a table. They also assume a brotherhood that will be tempted to renovate the inn and file a deed.

The Early Rule’s horror of touching money is not generic anti-greed. It is a refusal of the medium of the mercantile revolution. The Later Rule keeps the nerve in writing and then invents the valve. Chapter 4 is the command; the “nevertheless” is the institution:

> “I strictly command all my brothers not to receive coins or money in any form, either personally or through intermediaries. Nevertheless, the ministers and custodians alone may take special care through their spiritual friends to provide for the needs of the sick and the clothing of the others according to places, seasons and cold climates, as they judge necessary, saving always that, as stated above, they do not receive coins or money.”
> — Later Rule, ch. 4

Read that twice. The first sentence is absolute. The second sentence keeps the first sentence and routes coin through friends and officers. Spirituals will later call the route a fiction. Conventuals will call it how a hospital gets blankets. Francis is already inside the contradiction while still alive. When later “spiritual friends” hold coin the friars will not touch, the sentence is kept and the nerve is at risk. You can defend the valve as inevitable. You cannot call it identical with the nerve.

Chapter 5 then pairs the refusal with work, so poverty does not become a performance of idleness:

> “Those brothers to whom the Lord has given the grace of working may work faithfully and devotedly so that, while avoiding idleness, the enemy of the soul, they do not extinguish the Spirit of holy prayer and devotion to which all temporal things must contribute. In payment for their work they may receive whatever is necessary for the bodily support of themselves and their brothers, excepting coin or money, and let them do this humbly as is becoming for servants of God and followers of most holy poverty.”
> — Later Rule, ch. 5

Idleness is named an enemy. Prayer is named the thing work must not kill. Pay is allowed as food and cloth, not as coin. That is a different economy from a résumé and a different economy from a professional beggar with a holy face.

Francis pairs poverty with work in his own last will as well:

> “And I worked with my hands, and I still desire to work; and I firmly wish that all my brothers give themselves to honest work… And when we are not paid for our work, let us have recourse to the Lord’s table, begging alms from door to door.”

Idle mendicancy is not the ideal. Neither is a craftsman who hoards. The pairing scrapes a modern résumé because modern identity is paid function. Francis wants example and the killing of idleness, not a wage as proof of worth.

Pride in patches is already infidelity. Poverty that looks in the mirror has married a different lady.

The public nude scene before Bishop Guido is a legal act: clothes returned, paternal authority refused, ecclesiastical covering accepted. Pietro is not a cartoon. He is a merchant watching inventory become masonry for a chapel that will not profit him. Family rupture is the cost, not a side effect.

Scholarly debate: Lambert and Burr map the later Spiritual/Conventual war onto this fuse. The Rule of 1223 says the brothers should appropriate nothing and go as pilgrims and strangers. The Testament is hotter: work with hands, no special privileges, lesser brothers. “Not appropriating” can mean two things. Individual non-ownership with a corporate body that still holds churches and books. Or no ownership at all — not even the order — only *usus*, use as borrowed. Francis’s practice leaned toward the second while the movement was small. Growth required roofs. The Spirituals insisted on absolute poverty, individual and corporate, and on *usus pauper* as a visible life, not a slogan. Some edges mixed that claim with Joachim of Fiore’s age of the Spirit, which made the papacy itself look like unrepented wealth. The Conventuals answered that preaching and study need schools, and schools need buildings, and a remnant too pure to preach has lost both mission and poverty. Nicholas III’s *Exiit Qui Seminat* (1279) tried a compromise: the order owns nothing; the pope holds property in trust; the brothers have use. Spirituals heard a legal fiction. Conventuals heard too much concession to rigorists. John XXII’s *Cum Inter Nonnullos* (1323) then declared it heretical to say that Christ and the apostles owned nothing. Marseille 1318 had already burned men who would not recant. Ockham fled to Louis of Bavaria and accused the pope. Francis is not a property theorist. He left a practice and a veto (“without gloss”) without a treatise. Both later parties quote him because the documents are genuinely tense. Picking a team too fast is how you stop reading. The modern split (OFM / Conventual / Capuchin) still names the argument. Capuchin reform (1525) is the nearest institutional cousin of the Spiritual nerve — and even Capuchins own corporately. The question “was Francis’s poverty compatible with institutional existence?” has no tidy answer. A dozen men can own nothing. Several thousand men need infrastructure. That is not a misunderstanding. It is the cost of being believed.

Hear the Rule’s opening again beside chapter 6, because poverty is not a side vow. “Observe the Holy Gospel… without anything of one’s own.” Gospel *is* the form; poverty is not an extra. When later canonists ask whether “anything” includes a book, a church, or a field held by the pope, they are not being silly. They are doing what growth forces. Francis’s counter is the command against gloss — which is itself a kind of law, an anti-commentary commentary. Burr’s Spirituals will treat that command as a third Testament. Conventuals will treat it as a dying man’s mood. John XXII will treat a related thesis as heresy. You need those names so “poverty” does not stay a poster.

The *Letter to the Rulers of the Peoples* belongs in this chapter because coin is political. A friar telling princes they will die is Lady Poverty speaking upstream. If poverty only disciplines friars, it is a hobby of the already-saved. Francis aims it at the people who mint the world he left. Harry 1.4 names the letter; the letter itself must be on the page, not only in a bibliography:

> “To all podestàs, and consuls, judges and governors, in whatever part of the world, and to all others to whom this letter may come, Brother Francis, your little and contemptible servant, wishes health and peace to you. Consider and see that the day of death draws nigh. I ask you, therefore, with such reverence as I can, not to forget the Lord on account of the cares and solicitudes of this world and not to turn aside from His commandments… And when the day of death comes, all that which they think they have shall be taken away from them. And the wiser and more powerful they may have been in this world, so much the greater torments shall they endure in hell. Wherefore, I strongly advise you, my lords, to put aside all care and solicitude and to receive readily the most holy Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ in holy commemoration of Him.”
> — *Letter to the Rulers of the Peoples* (c. 1220; Robinson 1905 from Wadding; Armstrong/Brady print the same letter)

Death, stripped possession, Eucharist. That is not a courtesy note. It is the mercantile palate addressed from below. The manuscript tradition is thin (Wadding 1623; Gonzaga’s report that John Parenti carried a copy into Spain). Thin is not fake. It is a reason to quote carefully and not to invent a longer harangue. What survives is already enough to wreck a civics unit that thinks Francis only scolded friars.

Joy (*laetitia*) is the test of fake poverty. Grimness as proof is a product. Holy foolishness is un-buyability. Later films that only laugh have failed the Testament’s anxiety. Later rigorists who never laugh have failed the Canticle. Hold both documents or you are in a faction, not a reading.

Clare’s stricter refusal of loopholes is not a cameo. A Francis booklet that never lets a woman be more radical than Francis is doing the order’s old work.

### Big Idea

Poverty, for Francis, is freedom from the securities that block trust and kinship. Institutional survival will try to keep the poetry and move the coin offstage. That move is the Franciscan problem, not a later accident.

### Practice

1. Fast from one medium of status for seven days (brand, app, update cadence). Journal what anxiety appears. Do not confuse this with being poor.
2. Rewrite Later Rule 6 as a policy for a student club that “owns nothing.” Where does the policy break on day three?
3. Two-chair debate: spiritual friends as wisdom vs. as fiction. Switch. Steelman the side you dislike.
4. Read Clare’s Form of Life (even a short excerpt) and list one place she is less compromising than the Later Rule.

### Reflect

What do you own that you could not return in a public square without feeling annihilated — and is that object, or is it you?

## Chapter 3: Few Words, Papal Bull, No Gloss

San Damiano’s voice — “repair my house, which as you see is falling completely into ruin” (Celano) — is the most famous sentence Francis may never have written down. The *Testament* gives faith in churches and a prayer of adoration instead:

> “We adore You, Lord Jesus Christ, here and in all Your churches throughout the whole world, and we bless You, because by Your holy cross You have redeemed the world.”

And the early prayer:

> “Most High, glorious God, enlighten the darkness of my heart and give me true faith, certain hope, and perfect charity, sense and knowledge, Lord, so that I may carry out Your holy and true command.”

Interior illumination, not masonry, is the petition. The masonry still happened. Literal obedience can be the doorway to a larger repair. Retrospective allegory (chapel = Church) is theologically apt and historically risky if it overwrites Francis’s silence.

The form of life, Later Rule ch. 1:

> “The Rule and life of the Friars Minor is this: to observe the Holy Gospel of Our Lord Jesus Christ, living in obedience, without anything of one’s own, and in chastity.”

Innocent III’s 1209 oral approval; Honorius III’s *Solet Annuere*, 29 November 1223. Between those dates the band becomes an international corporation. Novitiate, examined preachers, permitted study “provided that you do not extinguish the spirit of prayer and devotion” (Letter to Anthony of Padua), spiritual friends, explicit papal obedience.

Francis resigns as minister general around 1220. He is a poor administrator who then, dying, tries to bind administrators:

> “I strictly command all my brothers, cleric and lay, by obedience, not to put any gloss or interpretation on the Rule or on these words, saying: ‘They should be understood in this way.’”

He also calls the Testament a remembrance, not another Rule. He wants the bull *and* the veto on commentary. History rarely honors both.

The hermitage rule (Martha/Mary rotation) is the structural critique of specialist holiness. Contemplation and dishes trade places. A movement that only preaches becomes a circus. A movement that only hides becomes a club.

Eucharistic maximalism wrecks the proto-Protestant reading. The *Testament* already says he sees “nothing corporally in this world of the Son of God except His most holy Body and Blood.” The *Letter to the Entire Order* (c. 1225) is a second witness, not a paraphrase of the same sentence. Dying, ill, he kisses the brothers’ feet on paper and aims them at the altar:

> “Wherefore, brothers, kissing your feet and with the charity of which I am capable, I conjure you all to show all reverence and all honor possible to the most holy Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, in whom the things that are in heaven and the things that are on earth are pacified and reconciled to Almighty God.”
> — *Letter to the Entire Order*

Repair does not mean a new denomination. It means adoration inside disappointing churches. A dropout Francis who “just loved Jesus and hated priests” collides with a deacon who will not let clergy become décor and will not found a parallel altar. The proto-Protestant retrieval is reception. Layer 1 will not fund a walkout.

Novitiate and examined preaching are the 1223 Rule’s answer to a band that had grown faster than memory. The Early Rule’s reception of brothers is almost a story: clothes to the poor, a tunic, entry. The Later Rule installs a year of probation and forbids preaching in a diocese against a bishop’s will, and forbids any brother to preach to the people unless examined and approved and given the office:

> “The brothers may not preach in the diocese of any bishop when he has opposed their doing so. And let none of the brothers dare to preach in any way to the people unless he has been examined and approved by the minister general of this fraternity and the office of preacher has been conferred upon him. I also admonish and exhort those brothers that when they preach their language be well-considered and chaste for the benefit and edification of the people, announcing to them vices and virtues, punishment and glory, with brevity, because our Lord when on earth kept his word brief.”
> — Later Rule, ch. 9

Charisma becomes an office. Short virtuous words, not a performance. That is not automatically betrayal. It is what canon law required of a real order. Francis’s heat in 1226 is the fear that office will eat the form. Heat is evidence. It is not a secret fourth Rule.

Scholarly debate: Dalarun and Thompson on how much San Damiano is Celano’s theology of a speaking Christ versus Francis’s own memory. Also: was the 1223 Rule a betrayal Francis was too ill to stop, or a survival he accepted? The Testament’s heat suggests acceptance was not peace. Heat is not the same as a secret counter-rule. He says it is not another Rule.

Portiuncula and San Damiano are leftover sacred space. Reform starts where budgets stopped. Clare’s later dwelling at San Damiano means the “house” becomes a women’s house — a fact men’s official lives under-narrate. Gender is not an appendix (Harry 7.4). If repair is only a male order with papal privileges, the house is still falling.

Study’s fuse-sentence to Anthony must be read as permission *and* limit. Bonaventure, Scotus, Ockham are not betrayals of a man who hated thought (misconception 5.2). They are what happens when prayer is told not to die during theology. The death of prayer in a classroom is still the named danger. Your AP year knows that danger without a habit.

Obedience to the Roman Church in the Later Rule is the reason Francis is not available as a mascot of pure dissent. It is also the reason later popes could bind the order against the Spirituals. You cannot cheer Innocent III’s yes in 1209 and treat John XXII as a random villain without watching the structure you cheered. That watch is history, not cynicism.

### Big Idea

Institutionalization is not the opposite of Francis. Gloss is. He sought papal confirmation and forbade interpretation. That pair is unstable on purpose.

### Practice

1. Gloss hunt: take a code you live under (honor code, team rules, family rule). Write the first clever interpretation people use. Write Francis’s “simply and purely” counter in one sentence. Then note the cost of the counter.
2. Time audit: one Sabbath-shaped hour with phones out of reach, then ordinary work with hands (dishes, repair). Short reflection: which one did you use to avoid the other?
3. Outline a hermitage rule for four people in exam week. Rotate Martha/Mary. See who refuses rotation.
4. Copy the adoration prayer into a church you do not “feel” is alive. Sixty seconds. Write what resistance appeared.

### Reflect

When you ask an authority to confirm your life, what have you already given them permission to interpret?

## Chapter 4: Vestige and the Preposition That Saves the Canticle

The Canticle is not a mood. It is a grammar. *Praised be You, my Lord, through…* The object of praise is God. Creatures are kin and channels. Brother Sun “bears a likeness of You, Most High One” — *likeness*, not identity.

> “Praised be You, my Lord, with all Your creatures, especially Sir Brother Sun, who is the day and through whom You give us light. And he is beautiful and radiant with great splendor; and bears a likeness of You, Most High One.”

Bonaventure will systematize *vestigia* (footprints) versus the *imago* in Christ. Francis does not write the *Itinerarium*. He introduces siblings, including weather that is not pretty, water that is “humble,” earth that “governs,” and later pardon-bearers and Sister Death.

Lynn White Jr. (1967) blamed Christian anthropocentrism for ecological ruin and offered Francis as exception. The exception thesis can become a way of keeping Christianity as the villain and Francis as an honorary pagan. Sorrell and Delio argue the Canticle is theocentric and Christological. *Laudato Si’* (2015) takes the opening as title, praises kinship over mere stewardship, and still refuses to detach the ethic from “the convictions of our faith” (§216) even while noting Francis is “much loved by non-Christians” (§10).

Integral ecology’s claim — one crisis, social and environmental — maps onto Francis’s single habit of refusing to treat a creature as object, whether leper or water. That mapping is historically defensible *and* incomplete. Francis ate meat the Rule allowed. He was not a carbon analyst. His contribution is orientation: gratitude rather than managerial panic.

Greccio (1223) is Incarnation staged in hay. Layer-4 wolf stories are parables with a late timestamp. Sort.

The Eucharist distinction again: altar is Body; sun is likeness. Collapse those and you get idolatry or magic. Separate them without kinship and you get a warehouse world.

Scholarly debate: can the kinship ethic be secularized? The Canticle’s every verse says no. A public square may still borrow the ethic. Borrowing is not identity. Honesty requires saying when the Lord has been edited out.

Fuller Canticle, because partial quotation is how the birdbath wins:

> “Praised be You, my Lord, through Sister Moon and the stars; in heaven You formed them clear and precious and beautiful.
> Praised be You, my Lord, through Brother Wind, and through the air, cloudy and serene, and every kind of weather through which You give sustenance to Your creatures.
> Praised be You, my Lord, through Sister Water, which is very useful and humble and precious and chaste.
> Praised be You, my Lord, through Brother Fire, through whom You light the night; and he is beautiful and playful and robust and strong.
> Praised be You, my Lord, through our Sister Mother Earth, who sustains and governs us, and who produces various fruits with colored flowers and herbs.”

*Governs* is the shock. Earth is not a warehouse under human CEO-ship. Francis is not writing Genesis out; he is refusing a reading of Genesis in which dominion means strip-mining a dead thing. White’s thesis needed an exception because his Christianity was already a villain. The better argument is that *some* Christianities loot, and this one sings. Sorrell’s monograph is the place to test whether “nature mystic” survives the preposition *through*.

Composition history matters for high readers: illness at San Damiano (first movement), civic pardon (second), deathbed (third). The poem is a life in three weathers, not a nature hike. If you only teach the elements, you have cut the human verses that make ecology moral rather than pretty.

The pantheist reading fails on the preposition. If Brother Sun *were* God, Francis would not need “likeness.” Sacrament is not identity. The sun is a vestige; Christ is the image. Collapse those and you get idolatry dressed as ecology. Keep them without kinship and you get a warehouse. *Laudato Si’* knows the difference: it names Francis “much loved by non-Christians” and still refuses to detach the ethic from “the convictions of our faith.” A public climate club may borrow kinship language. Borrowing is not the Canticle. Honesty is saying when the Most High has been edited out. White needed an exception to a Christian villain story. He did not need an honorary druid. Sorrell’s monograph is the test of whether “nature mystic” survives *through*. If it does not, stop using the birdbath as a primary source.

Weather in the Canticle is not pretty. Brother Wind includes “cloudy and serene, and every kind of weather.” Sister Water is “useful and humble and precious and chaste” — four adjectives that refuse both luxury and waste. Brother Fire is “playful and robust and strong,” which is not a campfire mood; it is a dangerous sibling you still praise. Sister Mother Earth “sustains and governs.” *Governs* is the insult to a managerial species. Francis is not writing Genesis out of the Bible. He is refusing a reading of dominion that treats earth as a dead inventory. Integral ecology’s claim (one crisis, poor and land) is historically defensible as a reception of that habit: the same refusal to treat a creature as object, whether leper or water. It is incomplete as a carbon policy. He ate meat the Rule allowed. He was not a climate model. Orientation is the gift: gratitude rather than panic, kinship rather than loot, vestige rather than idol.

Greccio (1223) belongs beside the Canticle, not as a nativity pageant. Celano’s crib is Incarnation staged in hay: God not embarrassed by straw. Layer-4 wolf stories are a different genre. Sort. The Eucharist distinction again, now as ecology’s brake: altar is Body; sun is likeness. Collapse those and you get magic or mush. Separate them without kinship and you get a warehouse world. High readers are old enough to keep three levels: Creator, vestige, sacrament. Dropping any one of them produces a Francis who cannot survive his own sentences. The Canticle’s last human verses — pardon-bearers, Sister Death — are not decorations on a nature poem. They are why ecology, in this grammar, is moral.

### Big Idea

Francis’s creation theology is sacramental, not pantheist: matter signifies because the Word made it and wore it. Ecology without that grammar is a different religion in his coat.

### Practice

## Chapter 5: The Greeting, the Thousandth Time, and the Sultan’s Unbaptized Return

Peace, in Francis, is not the unattributed poster poem. It is a revealed greeting:

> “And the Lord revealed to me that I should say the greeting, ‘The Lord give you peace,’ which I previously did not use.”

It flattens rank. It echoes John 20. It is ordinary enough to become cheap if you let it.

The Canticle’s pardon verse was a civic intervention between podestà and bishop:

> “Praised be You, my Lord, through those who give pardon for Your love, and bear infirmity and tribulation. Blessed are those who endure in peace, for by You, Most High, shall they be crowned.”

Pardon costs. Peace is a crown, not a nap. The *Letter to a Minister* orders a thousandth reception after a fall. That is not the absence of standards. It is a refusal of exile as the first tool.

Damietta, 1219. Fifth Crusade, legate Pelagius, starving city, divided camp. Francis appalled by plunder-as-piety. Early Rule 16:

> “And the brothers who go among the Saracens… can live among them spiritually… or, when they see it pleases God, announce the word of God.”

The *or* is the ethics. Francis chooses announcement, expects martyrdom or conversion, gets neither. Celano: the sultan listened “with great attention.” Gifts refused. Safe return. No baptism. Later fire-ordeal and secret conversion fail the early-source test (Tolan’s reception history is the map of the projections).

Three readings, all partly sourced: missionary, encounter, prophetic protest. A medieval Catholic who thinks Islam false can still refuse a sword and still fail. Afterward he warns against an attack; the army attacks; disaster. That does not make him Clausewitz. It makes him un-usable as a mascot of victory.

Al-Kamil’s courtesy is not a transcript we possess. Celano is a canonization writer (Gregory IX, 1228). Hold the gap.

Home-front letters (to the Order, to ministers, to rulers) show the same grammar: Eucharist, priests, temporary power, brotherhood not office. If Damietta is brave and the breakfast table is contempt, you have quoted a scene, not a life.

Scholarly debate: Tolan vs. traditional missionary hagiography vs. modern dialogical retrieval. Powell on crusade mechanics. The productive tension is that Francis’s Christocentrism is not canceled by his crossing, and his crossing is not canceled by his Christocentrism. Either cancellation is anachronism with a halo.

James Powell’s *Anatomy of a Crusade* keeps Damietta from becoming a fable: siege, hunger, legate’s delays, strategic fantasy of swapping Egypt for Jerusalem. Francis’s walk does not alter the machine. That is the point. Prophetic acts often fail as policy and succeed as un-erasable pictures. The Church cannot bury the lesser brother in the tent. It can, and did, keep crusading. Holding both is adulthood.

Do not baptize al-Kamil with later legend. Do not baptize Francis as a pluralist. The Earlier Rule’s two paths are medieval Catholic tools for a minority in *dar al-Islam* and for preaching. Francis picks preaching and still lives. Courtesy is not conversion. Conversion is not courtesy. The “great attention” sentence is doing canonization work. It may also be true. You are old enough to keep a sentence in that double state without exploding.

Letters to rulers and custodes (Eucharist reserved with dignity) show peacemaking as reverence, not only as geopolitics. A camp that gambles under a cross is a liturgical crisis. Francis’s disgust is theological: the Body is treated as a mascot of war. That disgust is available to you without a Nile.

The rulers letter, already blocked in chapter 2 as a poverty document, belongs again here as a peace document. Temporary power, evening praise, Body and Blood, account at judgment. Francis does not write a just-war treatise. He writes a reminder that lords die. Early Rule 16 is the only grammar he authorized for walking among “Saracens”: live among them spiritually, subject to them for God’s sake, *or* announce the word if it pleases God. He picks announcement, expects martyrdom or conversion, gets neither. The *or* is not modern pluralism. It is a medieval Catholic pair of tools. Do not baptize al-Kamil. Do not baptize Francis as a UN delegate. Keep Christocentrism and the crossing on the same page. Either cancellation is anachronism with a halo.

The Peace Prayer (“Lord, make me an instrument of your peace”) is a twentieth-century text. It is not in Armstrong/Brady. It is not in Celano. It is not a Layer-1 veto. Use the revealed greeting instead. Using the poster as “Francis” is recruitment. The *Fioretti* wolf is Layer 4: a parable with a late timestamp, labeled legend in this booklet, not promoted to a city-council minutes. Peace in Francis is practiced: greeting, costly pardon, a thousandth reception, a walk that converts no one.

### Big Idea

Franciscan peace is practiced: same greeting, costly pardon, restored brothers, and a documented walk into an enemy camp that converts no one and blesses no siege.

## Chapter 6: Final Seal, Sister Death, and the War Over a Will

La Verna, September 1224. Celano’s seraph, nails, side. Companions see. Francis hides. Pain, not branding. First widely attested stigmatic. After him, more. Dante: “from whom Christ took the final seal” (*Paradiso* XI).

Francis’s own near-silence on the wounds, beside loud Eucharistic speech, should control interpretation. He will not make his body the sacrament. The altar already is.

Mechanism: miracle, psychosomatic Passion-imitation, chronic disease (malaria, malnutrition, possible tuberculoid lesions). 1224 has no lab. Thompson will not let you be glib. Vauchez tracks sanctity’s transformation: holiness becomes bodily participation, not only wonder-working. Meaning can be Christological while mechanism stays open. Sneering at either inquiry is cheap.

Sister Death, last layer of the Canticle, deathbed at the Portiuncula, 3 October 1226:

> “Praised be You, my Lord, through our Sister Bodily Death, from whom no living man can escape. Woe to those who die in mortal sin. Blessed are those whom death will find in Your most holy will, for the second death shall do them no harm.”

Woe and blessing. Not slush. The only surviving autograph is not a wound-report. It is a blessing on a scrap, still in Assisi:

> “Blessing and all good, the Lord give you. Brother Francis.”
> — Blessing of Brother Leo (autograph, c. 1224–1225)

Master refused in a signature. Esser on the autograph is the scholarly reminder: the one scrap in his hand chooses “Brother.” The *Praises of God*, written for Leo on the same season’s parchment, is not a tag. It is a list. Humility is a divine name, which is why display would be a different god:

> “You are holy, Lord God, the only God, who does wonderful things. You are strong. You are great. You are the Most High. You are love. You are wisdom. You are humility. You are patience.”
> — *Praises of God* (for Brother Leo)

If humility is what God *is*, stigmata as spectacle would contradict the theology of the wounds. Hiding is doctrine in gloves, not only embarrassment.

Then the civil war. *Without gloss* versus thousands who need roofs. Nicholas III, *Exiit Qui Seminat* (1279): papal trust, use without ownership. John XXII, *Cum Inter Nonnullos* (1323): heresy to say Christ and the apostles owned nothing. Marseille 1318. Ockham in exile. OFM / Conventual / Capuchin still name the split. Both sides had a case. Francis was not a systematic jurist of property. Growth created the contradiction; his veto made it explosive.

Return to the poverty controversy at the end of the life, because the controversy is not a footnote to the wounds. It is what the wounds could not settle. Spirituals claimed Christ and the apostles owned nothing, neither individually nor corporately, so every friary and library owned by the order was a Gospel betrayal. Conventuals claimed the Apostolic life includes preaching, and preaching on a continent requires books and roofs, held if need be by the pope in trust. *Exiit* satisfied neither camp. *Cum Inter Nonnullos* ended the legal fiction by making a poverty thesis heresy. Men burned. A Franciscan philosopher accused a pope. That is the Anthony permission under maximum stress, not “anti-intellectual Francis.” Chapter 2 gave you the fuse. Chapter 6 gives you the explosion. Do not collapse them. The founder’s last words were formally overruled: the Testament was judged not a legal document. Charismatic authority lost to institutional authority inside the house he founded. That loss is part of the history, not a later misunderstanding.

Hagiography problem (your Layer 1–5): Bonaventure’s *Legenda Maior* unifies a saint and sidelines messier lives. *Fioretti* makes a folk hero. Modern *Early Documents* (Armstrong, Hellmann, Short) try to let the layers argue. The closer to Francis’s voice, the more anxious, sacramental, commanding, un-cute. Conversion, San Damiano, Damietta, and La Verna all change shape as you climb the ladder. Celano writes to support a canonization. Bonaventure writes to unify an order already splitting over poverty. The *Fioretti* writers write to make a lovable lay saint. Thompson writes to weigh. If you only read Layer 4, you will love him and not know him. If you only read Layer 5, you will know about him and never hear him.

Open questions (not closed here): Clare and gender; reluctant authority; crusade compromise; limits of Christocentric “universalism.” Clare’s Privilege of Poverty arrived on her deathbed. Francis supported her spiritually and did not build her the male order’s legal house. He resigned ~1220; the Testament reads anxious. He walked to the sultan with crusade sanction and did not denounce the war as such. He honored al-Kamil without becoming a pluralist. A statue that hides those is a recruitment tool.

Mechanism of the stigmata, without a courtroom close: the marks were real in the only sense a historian can use — early sources, companions, persistence. Thomas of Celano describes black marks and a side that often bled: more restrained than later art. Francis does not write the spectacle. Interpretation is theological: conformity becoming participation; seraph as burning love united to crucifixion. Medical options stay open: psychosomatic Passion-attention, pre-existing disease (malaria, malnutrition, possible tuberculoid lesions), miracle. Self-infliction is possible in theory and poorly matched to the sources. 1224 has no lab. Meaning does not wait on mechanism. Glibness — credulous or sneering — is the only forbidden affect. Vauchez tracks the shift: sanctity proven in a living body, not only at a tomb. Dante’s seal is meaning, not a witness to flesh.

Celano (*Vita Prima* 94–95) and Bonaventure (*Legenda Maior* 13) are the stigmata’s narrative engines. Brother Leo’s independent line of testimony matters because official lives have jobs. Esser on the autograph reminds you that the one scrap in Francis’s hand is a blessing, not a wound-report. He would not write the spectacle. You should notice.

*Praises of God* for Leo — “You are humility” — is the metaphysics under “Brother Francis.” If humility is a divine name, then the stigmata as display would be a contradiction. Hiding is not embarrassment only. It is doctrine in gloves.

Afterlife of the will: *Exiit Qui Seminat* tries to save radical poverty with a legal fiction elegant enough to satisfy neither camp. *Cum Inter Nonnullos* ends the fiction by sacramentalizing property into Christ’s own life — a theological nuclear option. Ockham’s flight shows a Franciscan intellectual tradition willing to accuse a pope. That is not “anti-intellectual Francis.” That is the Anthony permission under maximum stress.

Dante’s *Paradiso* XI is reception at genius level: Poverty as bride, Francis as lover, the seal as close. Use Dante as theology of meaning, not as a witness to 1224 flesh. Vauchez on afterlife of a medieval saint is the scholarly version of the same warning.

### Big Idea

Francis’s end matches his beginning: unclutching, even of reputation and breath. The centuries fight because the gift was real enough to incorporate and therefore to betray.

## For the Grown-Up Reader

High-band sources: Armstrong and Brady, *Francis and Clare: The Complete Works* (Paulist, 1982); Celano, *Vita Prima*; Bonaventure, *Legenda Maior*; Augustine Thompson, *Francis of Assisi: A New Biography* (2012); Jacques Dalarun, *The Misadventures of Francis of Assisi*; John Tolan, *Saint Francis and the Sultan* (2009); David Burr, *The Spiritual Franciscans* (2001); Malcolm Lambert, *Franciscan Poverty*; Roger Sorrell, *St. Francis of Assisi and Nature* (1988); Ilia Delio, *A Franciscan View of Creation*; Pope Francis, *Laudato Si’* (2015); Dante, *Paradiso* XI; Lynn White Jr., *Science* 155 (1967); André Vauchez, *Francis of Assisi* (2012). Peace Prayer: not Francis. Wolf of Gubbio: *Fioretti*, not Layer 1. Stigmata: early testimony, open mechanism. No pastoral or medical advice. No family names.

The single Research Prompt (ch. 1) privileges Testament over later set pieces unless you can argue otherwise with pages. That is a historical method, not a piety.

### A longer read of the Testament (use with chapters 1–3)

Francis’s last document is short enough to read aloud in one sitting and dense enough to wreck a semester of slogans. Read it as a dying founder’s architecture, not as a memoir with feelings attached.

On churches and the cross, he does not mention a voice in a ruined chapel. He mentions a habit of adoration. That habit is already a theology of presence: Christ is not privately owned by the enthusiast. He is in “all Your churches throughout the whole world.” A spirituality that cannot enter a disappointing building is not his.

On brothers: “no one showed me what I should do, but the Most High Himself revealed to me that I should live according to the form of the Holy Gospel. And I had this written down simply and in few words, and the Lord Pope confirmed it for me.” Three agents: Most High, Francis as scribe of few words, pope as confirmer. Remove any one and you get a different movement: pure interiority, pure founder-cult, or pure curia. Francis wants the triangle. The triangle will not stay equilateral.

On work and the Lord’s table, he blocks two medieval (and modern) clerical vices: the man who will not labor, and the man who will not ask. Alms after unpaid work is not a social program. It is a wager that God still feeds. Test the wager and you will discover how much of your courage is just a grocery store.

On gloss: the command is issued “by obedience” to “cleric and lay.” Learned brothers are named first in the temptation. The gloss is the clerical specialty. Francis has already permitted Anthony to teach. He is not burning books. He is forbidding the move that says “what this really means is…” whenever the Rule costs. High-school essay culture is a gloss machine. Notice the resemblance without self-hatred. Then write anyway, and mark your glosses as yours, not as his.

On priests: “I see nothing corporally in this world of the Son of God except His most holy Body and Blood, which they receive and which they alone administer to others.” This is the sentence that ends the dropout myth. It is also the sentence that makes Francis dangerous to a Church that uses sacrament as décor. If the Body is the only corporal presence, then war-camps, markets, and palaces are all being measured against an altar. That is not quietism.

Read the Testament twice: once for the leper, once for the triangle of Most High / few words / pope. If your notes from the two readings do not fight each other, you read too fast.

### A longer read of Later Rule 1 and 6 (use with chapters 2–3)

Chapter 1’s “Friars Minor” is a class insult turned into a name. Lesser. Not a branding agency’s “humility.” A refusal of *majores*. Assisi taught him the word.

Chapter 6’s pilgrims and strangers is Exodus language without a map app. Confidence in alms is either faith or a scam. Francis knows both exist. He still commands confidence. That command will be abused by idle men. He still will not make wages the proof of a calling. Your internship culture will find this either romantic or offensive. Both reactions are data.

Spiritual friends, once introduced, become the whole later controversy in seed. If you can already see how a valve becomes a pipeline, you are ready for Burr. If you cannot, you are still treating poverty as a feeling.

### Damietta without the soundtrack (use with chapter 5)

Remove the film score. You have a barefoot deacon, one companion, a year-plus siege, a cultured sultan, a papal legate, gambling soldiers, a Rule that already imagined this walk, a safe return, no baptism, a warning ignored, a later library of fire and secret conversions. Tolan’s service is to show you your century in the story. What does our century need Francis to have been? Dialoguer, because we fear religion. Missionary, because we fear relativism. Pacifist, because we fear our own wars. He is not hired to complete our sentences. The Early Rule’s *or* is the only sentence he actually authorized for the walk. Stay near it.

### La Verna without the poster (use with chapter 6)

Remove the glow. You have a sick man on a Tuscan mountain, Passion devotion as a medieval technology of attention, a vision-report from someone else’s pen, wounds companions said they saw, gloves, two years of pain, a hiddenness that fits “You are humility,” a poet later calling it a seal, doctors later offering mechanisms, a Church later minting a type of saint. You may believe. You may doubt. You may not be glib. Glib is the only forbidden scholarly affect in this booklet.

### Four misconceptions, argued (not cartooned)

**Nature worshipper.** The Canticle’s *through* and *likeness* are the proof. If Francis were a pantheist, Brother Sun would not “bear a likeness”; he would be the thing itself. Sacramental cosmology is not paganism with better PR. The modern ecological retrieval is historically defensible (Canticle is Layer 1; *Laudato Si’* knows the difference between kinship and stewardship) and becomes indefensible the moment the Most High is cut. Lynn White needed an exception; he did not need an honorary druid.

**Anti-intellectual.** Letter to Anthony; permitted study in the Later Rule; the later tradition of Bonaventure, Scotus, Ockham. The named danger is study that kills prayer, not thought. The Spirituals’ later apocalyptic currents (Joachimist edges) are not “Francis hated schools.” They are what happens when *without gloss* meets a university world. 5.2 is a slander that makes pious people proud of not reading.

**Medieval hippie.** Asceticism, “Brother Ass” as a name for the body, hidden stigmata, work requirement, obedience, chastity. Joy is real and is not the 1960s. *Laetitia* that refuses grimness is not a refusal of discipline. If your Francis never fasts, never commands, never hides a wound, you have a costume.

**Proto-Protestant.** Eucharistic maximalism, faith in priests, papal confirmation sought and cited, adoration in “all Your churches.” Repair is intramural. The Reformation will have its own Francis retrievals; they are reception. Layer 1 will not fund a walkout.

### What “without gloss” cannot mean

It cannot mean “no thought.” Anthony may teach. It cannot mean “no pope.” The Rule is bullated. It cannot mean “no development.” Thousands arrived. It means: when the cost appears, do not translate it into a cheaper sentence and call the cheaper sentence his. That is a moral of reading as much as a moral of poverty. It applies to this booklet. If these chapters have made Francis easier than the *Testament*, throw the chapter away and keep the *Testament*.

### A note on Clare, Bonaventure, Thérèse

This pack is Francis only (WIP: one figure). Clare will test whether a woman can out-poor the founder and still be told she is a helper. Bonaventure will test whether intellect can be a form of lesser-brotherhood or only a career. Thérèse, later and Carmelite, will test whether littleness can survive a different century without Assisi’s streets. Do not steal their booklets to pad this one. Do name them so Francis does not eat the family.

### Incarnation, Brother Ass, and solidarity

Francis’s creation theology is not a nature hobby bolted onto a separate Jesus hobby. The manger and the cross are the same claim: God did not save the world by remaining an idea. Greccio’s animals are not cute. They are a polemic against a spirituality too refined for straw. The stigmata, whatever their mechanism, are the same polemic at the other end of the life: not too refined for wounds.

He called his body Brother Ass — a working animal you load and also do not romanticize. Asceticism that hates the body is not his. Asceticism that spoils the body to impress heaven is the pride he feared. The *Testament*’s sweetness of *body* after the leper is the positive form of the same anthropology. Conversion happens in nerves, stomach, skin. A high-school dualism that treats “spiritual” as the opposite of “physical” cannot read him.

Solidarity with the poor is proximity, not charity-as-distance. Lady Poverty without lepers is an aesthetic. Lepers without Lady Poverty can become a volunteer hour that leaves the shop standing. Francis insists on both, which is why modern readers keep trying to split him into a social worker and a mystic. Split him and you can hire half of him. He did not offer halves.

### How to argue without recruiting him

You will want Francis on your side of a present fight: climate, war, inequality, church reform, interfaith, animal ethics, anti-intellectual populism, traditional liturgy. He will almost fit. “Almost” is the trap. The method of this booklet is to let Layer 1 veto the recruitment. If your cause needs him to have written the Peace Prayer, you are recruiting. If it needs the sultan baptized, you are recruiting. If it needs him to hate priests, you are recruiting. If it needs him to be harmless, you are recruiting.

Recruitment is not the same as reception. *Laudato Si’* is reception that keeps the grammar. A garden statue is recruitment that drops the grammar. Dante is reception. The *Fioretti* is devotion that must be labeled. Learn the difference or you will go to college and call every citation scholarship.

### Briefs: joy, preaching, time

Holy foolishness is the refusal to let the market set the face you wear. It is not a personality type. Performative quirkiness can be a brand, which is to say a possession. Test joy this way: does it survive hiddenness? Francis hid wounds. A joy that needs an audience is already a coin.

Short virtuous words, not elaborate sermons. Illiterate brothers should not fake a pulpit. Examined brothers may preach. Anthony’s permission sits in the middle with a knife: theology, provided prayer is not extinguished. Write your essays as if that knife were real.

Francis is not your contemporary. 1198 is not 2015. Al-Kamil is not a UN delegate. Anachronism is a form of gloss. The opposite vice is antiquarianism that refuses any living claim. The Canticle still names your water. The Testament still names your delay. Live claims must survive the veto of difference.
